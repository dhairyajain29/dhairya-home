## Does it fit?

The rear roof union is approximately **190.8 sq ft / 17.7 m²** before allowing for any roof obstructions or access. The six panel bodies occupy about **12.25 m²** in their own planes.

The illustrated panel rectangles fit this L shape without covering the OTS or the approach. The two panels above the washroom have only about an inch of spare width near the outer roof edges. That is a geometric fit, **not an approved mounting clearance**. Rails, supports, roof edges, drainage, shading and a maintenance route may require a different arrangement.

The national rooftop-solar FAQ gives a broad guide of about **10 m² per kWp**, with actual area depending on panel efficiency and placement. Compact panels can need less panel area, but that does not eliminate space needed for a workable installation. [MNRE rooftop solar FAQ, section 1.3](https://solarrooftop.pmsuryaghar.gov.in/pdf/faq_national_portal2024021301.pdf).

The final installer should coordinate the layout with the actual roof and supporting structure. The drawing does not specify anchors, ballast, reinforcement, wiring, protection equipment or permissible roof loads.

## Will 3 kW run the whole house?

**3 kW describes rated generating power; kWh describes electricity used or produced over time.** For example, a 1 kW appliance running for one hour consumes 1 kWh, commonly called one electricity unit.

Solar production varies with sunlight, time of day, season, weather, dirt and shadows. Household use can peak when solar generation is low. Storage can move some daytime generation to later hours. [U.S. Department of Energy: Solar Energy and Storage Basics](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/solar-integration-solar-energy-and-storage-basics).

A regular grid-connected system normally relies on the electricity grid and does not automatically power the home during an outage. Backup operation needs a suitable inverter/system arrangement and storage. [U.S. Department of Energy: Solar and Resilience Basics](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/solar-and-resilience-basics).

To assess your house’s needs, the useful inputs are:

- Monthly electricity units from recent bills, including summer use.
- Number and size of ACs, geysers and other major appliances, and which operate together.
- Whether your aim is lower bills, backup for essential appliances, or whole-house backup, and for how many hours.
- House location and roof orientation/shading.

Those details are not yet confirmed, so this package includes **a 3 kW solar concept, not a guarantee of energy independence or a yield estimate**. It does not assume an Indore location, a south-facing roof or a battery capacity.
